# Mathematical framework for model and controller design

This is the standing mathematical reference for every new policy, model,
controller, training objective, and evaluation in this repository. Read it before
designing a model; cite the relevant sections in its design notes. Verify
implementation details against the linked sources when those sources change.

**Scope:** local starter-kit implementation at commit
`23a4560df0e2fe61054dd21066fa0c4b5da2c79c`, checked on 2026-10-19.
Numerical examples describe shipped configurations, not universal constants for
unseen buildings. Section 10 records the limits of hidden-evaluator verification.

## 1. Decision problem and admissible models

For scenario $s$, building metadata $m_s$, physical state $x_t$, disturbance
$w_t$, observation $o_t$, continuous action $a_t$, binary action $b_t$, and
controller memory $z_t$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
u_t&=(a_t,b_t),\\
(a_t,b_t,z_{t+1})&=\pi_\theta(m_s,o_t,z_t), \tag{1}\\
x_{t+1}&=F_s(x_t,u_t,w_t),\\
o_t&=h_s(x_t,w_t).
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\mathcal F_t=\sigma(m_s,o_0,u_0,o_1,\ldots,u_{t-1},o_t),
\qquad u_t\text{ is }\mathcal F_t\text{-measurable}. \tag{2}
$$

For randomized policies, include an independent controller random seed in
$\mathcal F_t$. Future disturbances and private simulator state are unavailable.

$$
x_t=(T_t^{\rm air},T_t^{\rm walls},T_t^{\rm mass},T_t^{\rm water},
      \text{equipment states},\ldots),
\qquad w_t=(T_t^{\rm outside},\text{occupancy},\text{weather},\ldots).
$$

$\theta$ is shared across scenarios; $z_t$ may adapt within a rollout. No neural
network architecture is prescribed:

$$
\pi_\theta\in
\{\text{PID},\text{adaptive control},\text{MPC},\text{neural policy},
  \text{hybrid controller},\ldots\}.
$$

The initial submitted V0 uses the shipped `PIDWaterResetPolicy` (`pid_water`).
Its measured performance is a baseline, not a mathematical requirement.

### Verified local sensor timing

A subsequent three-step simulator probe verified that the zone-temperature
register is refreshed at the start of a physical step, before the thermal field
advances. If $T_t^{physical}$ denotes the actual grid-averaged zone temperature at
decision $t$, then, after initialization, the local observation behaves as

$$
o_t^{zone\ temperature}=\mathcal N(T_{t-1}^{physical}),\qquad
T_{t+1}^{physical}=F_T(x_t,u_t,w_t).
$$

Thus the temperature consequence of $u_t$ appears in $o_{t+2}$, and system
identification must align those delayed labels. The overall state $x_t$ in (1)
can include these sensor registers, preserving the observation formulation.
Weather and water observations need not have the same delay; do not shift the
whole observation vector uniformly.

In a building-8/Phoenix probe, a maximum-flow cooling action changed the physical
zone temperature from 294 K to 289.354 K while the returned zone sensor still
reported 294 K. The next returned sensor reported 289.354 K. The saved probe is
[here](experiments/observation_delay.json). This verifies the local implementation;
the hidden SDK's timing has not been independently checked.

Sources: [sensor refresh and thermal step](smart_buildings/smart_control/simulator/simulator_flexible_floor_plan.py),
[VAV sensor register](smart_buildings/smart_control/simulator/vav.py),
[observation request](smart_buildings/smart_control/simulator/simulator_building.py).

## 2. Exact local scoring objective

For $S$ evaluated scenarios and rollout horizon $H_s$:

$$
R_s(\pi)=\sum_{t=0}^{H_s-1}r_{s,t},\qquad
\widehat y_s=\Phi_s(\pi)=\frac{R_s(\pi)}{H_s},\qquad y_s=0.
$$

The mean-absolute-error interpretation is

$$
J(\pi)=\frac1S\sum_{s=1}^{S}|\widehat y_s-y_s|,
\qquad \min_\pi J(\pi). \tag{3}
$$

$\widehat y_s$ is generated by executing the policy in the simulator. It is not
a freely chosen regression prediction.

In the shipped reward configuration:

$$
r_{s,t}=-\ell_{s,t}
=-\left(0.6C_{s,t}+0.2E_{s,t}+0.2G_{s,t}\right)\leq0. \tag{4}
$$

Consequently, for full local episodes:

$$
J(\pi)=\frac1S\sum_s\frac1{H_s}
\sum_t\left(0.6C_{s,t}+0.2E_{s,t}+0.2G_{s,t}\right).
$$

$$
\Delta t=300\ {\rm s},\quad H_s=8640,
\quad H_s\Delta t=30\ {\rm days},\quad \gamma_{\rm score}=1.
$$

Every step contributes, including the beginning of the episode. There is no
baseline-return subtraction or baseline-return ratio in this metric. A training
algorithm may choose a discount factor, but that does not redefine the score.

**Local implementation detail:** `score_leaderboard.py` divides returns by the
fixed constant `8640`. Even its `--baselines self` mode retains that denominator.
Short-rollout results therefore require separately computed $R_s/H_s$ and must
not be presented as full-episode leaderboard estimates.

Sources: [local score](score_leaderboard.py),
[reward](smart_buildings/smart_control/reward/setpoint_energy_carbon_regret.py),
[example configuration](smart_buildings/training_scenarios/building_train_1/fairbanks_1/config.gin).

## 3. Comfort term

For zone $i$, let $T_{i,t}$ be its sensed air temperature in kelvin,
$[L_{i,t},U_{i,t}]$ its current setpoint band, and $n_{i,t}\geq0$ its average
occupancy during the reward interval. Define

$$
d_{i,t}=\max\{0,L_{i,t}-T_{i,t},T_{i,t}-U_{i,t}\},
\qquad N_t=\sum_{i=1}^{V}n_{i,t}.
$$

$$
\phi(d)=
\begin{cases}
d/w,&0\leq d\leq w,\\
1+((d-w)/w)^2,&d>w,
\end{cases}
\qquad w=5\ {\rm K}. \tag{5}
$$

$$
\phi(0)=0,\quad\phi(1)=0.2,\quad\phi(5)=1,
\quad\phi(10)=2,\quad\phi(15)=5.
$$

For the shipped minimum-productivity setting $v_{\min}=0$:

$$
C_t=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle\frac{\sum_i n_{i,t}\phi(d_{i,t})}{N_t},&N_t>0,\\
0,&N_t=0.
\end{cases} \tag{6}
$$

The underlying productivity calculation is

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal P_t&=v_{\max}\frac{\Delta t}{3600}
                 \sum_i n_{i,t}(1-\phi(d_{i,t})),\\
\mathcal P_t^{\max}&=v_{\max}\frac{\Delta t}{3600}N_t,\qquad
\mathcal P_t^{\min}=v_{\min}\frac{\Delta t}{3600}N_t,\\
g_t^{\rm productivity}&=
\frac{\mathcal P_t-\mathcal P_t^{\min}}
     {\mathcal P_t^{\max}-\mathcal P_t^{\min}}-1=-C_t.
\end{aligned}
$$

Here $v_{\max}=300\ {\rm USD/(person\,hour)}$. More generally, for
$N_t>0$ and $v_{\max}>v_{\min}$:

$$
C_t=\frac{v_{\max}}{v_{\max}-v_{\min}}
     \frac{\sum_i n_{i,t}\phi(d_{i,t})}{N_t}.
$$

Example bands in the shipped configuration:

$$
[L,U]_{\rm comfort}=[294,297]\ {\rm K},\qquad
[L,U]_{\rm eco}=[289,298]\ {\rm K}.
$$

Use live observations and schedule metadata for the actual band. Comfort is a
soft cost; $L_{i,t}\leq T_{i,t}\leq U_{i,t}$ is not a hard feasibility constraint.
Unoccupied zones have zero immediate occupancy-weighted comfort contribution,
but their temperatures affect future states. The function $\phi$ is continuous,
nondecreasing, and unbounded; it is not globally convex because its derivative
drops from $1/w$ to $0$ at $w$.

Sources: [comfort and normalization](smart_buildings/smart_control/reward/setpoint_energy_carbon_regret.py),
[zone productivity calculation](smart_buildings/smart_control/reward/base_setpoint_energy_carbon_reward.py).

## 4. Energy and carbon terms

Let $P_{e,t},P_{g,t}\geq0$ be electrical and gas power in watts;
$\alpha_{e,t},\alpha_{g,t}$ energy prices in USD/J; and
$\beta_{e,t},\beta_{g,t}$ emission factors in kg/J. For coefficients constant
over an interval, monetary cost $K_t^{\rm cost}$ and carbon $B_t^{\rm carbon}$ are

$$
K_t^{\rm cost}=\Delta t(\alpha_{e,t}P_{e,t}+\alpha_{g,t}P_{g,t}),
\quad
B_t^{\rm carbon}=\Delta t(\beta_{e,t}P_{e,t}+\beta_{g,t}P_{g,t}).
$$

When an interval crosses a tariff boundary, use the interval-integrated
coefficients returned by the cost model. With reference power scales
$\overline P_e,\overline P_g$:

$$
E_t=\frac{\alpha_{e,t}P_{e,t}+\alpha_{g,t}P_{g,t}}
          {\alpha_{e,t}\overline P_e+\alpha_{g,t}\overline P_g},
\quad
G_t=\frac{\beta_{e,t}P_{e,t}+\beta_{g,t}P_{g,t}}
          {\beta_{e,t}\overline P_e+\beta_{g,t}\overline P_g}. \tag{7}
$$

Each term is defined as zero when its denominator is nonpositive, as in the
implementation. For `building_train_1`:

$$
\overline P_e=320000\ {\rm W},\qquad
\overline P_g=820000\ {\rm W}.
$$

These are normalization scales, not physical consumption caps. There is no
clipping to one: $E_t>1$, $G_t>1$, and $\ell_t>1$ are possible.

Equivalently, when denominators are positive:

$$
\ell_t=0.6C_t+\lambda_{e,t}P_{e,t}+\lambda_{g,t}P_{g,t}, \tag{8}
$$

$$
\lambda_{q,t}=
\frac{0.2\alpha_{q,t}}
     {\alpha_{e,t}\overline P_e+\alpha_{g,t}\overline P_g}
+\frac{0.2\beta_{q,t}}
      {\beta_{e,t}\overline P_e+\beta_{g,t}\overline P_g},
\qquad q\in\{e,g\}.
$$

Electrical demand includes fan power, cooling electricity, and water-pump power;
heat-pump electricity is included where that equipment is configured. Shipped
cost models use hour-dependent electricity prices/emissions, weekday/weekend
electricity pricing, monthly gas prices, and a constant gas emission factor.

Source: [reward and energy accounting](smart_buildings/smart_control/reward/setpoint_energy_carbon_regret.py).

## 5. Dataset and runtime inputs

The shipped scenario set is

$$
\mathcal D_{\rm train}=\{s_{b,c}:b\in\{1,\ldots,6\},
\ c\in\{\text{Fairbanks, Denver, Atlanta, Phoenix, Singapore}\}\},
\quad |\mathcal D_{\rm train}|=30.
$$

Each building/station pair uses its own recorded 30-day strip. This is not five
identical weather windows reused across all six buildings. Geometry, materials,
equipment, schedules, and disturbances define an interactive simulator:

| File/input | Mathematical role |
| --- | --- |
| Building/scenario YAML | Geometry, material and equipment parameters |
| `config.gin` | Scenario bindings, dynamics and reward parameters |
| `floor_plan.npy` | Numerical geometry grid |
| `zone_map.npy` | Numerical zone-construction grid; do not assume entries are zone IDs |
| Weather CSV | Time-indexed external disturbances |
| Runtime metadata $m_s$ | Index maps, normalizers, geometry, assignments, schedule |
| Runtime observation $o_t$ | Current observable state information |

For one inspected building-1 scenario, both grids are `float64`, shape
`(606, 339)`, with codes $\{0,1,2\}$. Interpret these arrays through the building
implementation rather than treating them as an already segmented zone-ID map.

Weather CSV columns include `Time`, `StationName`, `Location`, `TempC`,
`DewPointC`, `WindspeedKmph`, `Humidity`, and `TempF`; some files also carry an
unnamed index column. Example timestamp: `20230101-0000`.

No optimal-action label dataset is supplied. Simulator experience has the form

$$
\mathcal E_\pi=\{(m_s,o_t,a_t,b_t,r_t,o_{t+1})\}_{s,t}.
$$

The metadata contract exposes these keys:

```text
n_discrete_action, n_continuous_action,
temp_indices, pressure_indices, water_indices, recirculation_indices,
zone_action_indices, zone_temp_indices, observation_field_names,
floor_plan, zone_map, ahu_zone_assignments, num_floors,
morning_start_hour, evening_start_hour, comfort_temp_window, eco_temp_window,
time_zone, holidays, observation_normalization_metadata,
action_normalization_metadata
```

$$
o_t\in\mathbb R^{d(m_s)},\qquad
o_{t,j}=\frac{v_{t,j}-\mu_j}{\sigma_j}
\quad\text{for fields with this configured normalizer}. \tag{9}
$$

Other fields may remain raw. Do not apply one universal normalization to every
entry. A measured building-1 reset begins:

| Observation field | Observed value | Decoded value where normalized |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| `VAV_1_reheat_valve_percentage_command` | $-1$ | $0$ |
| `VAV_1_supply_air_damper_percentage_command` | $-0.8000000119$ | $\simeq0.1$ |
| `VAV_1_supply_air_flowrate_setpoint` | $2$ | Raw field |
| `VAV_1_zone_air_cooling_temperature_setpoint_sensor` | $297$ | $297\ {\rm K}$ |
| `VAV_1_zone_air_heating_temperature_setpoint_sensor` | $294$ | $294\ {\rm K}$ |
| `VAV_1_zone_air_temperature_sensor` | $-0.200000003$ | $295+5(-0.2)=294\ {\rm K}$ |

Other observations include per-AHU temperatures, pressure, flow, recirculation,
and run status; water-system readings; building occupancy; comfort flags; outside
temperature; and cyclic time features. Runtime metadata does not supply a
building/station label to use as a lookup key.

The local evaluator explicitly sets occupancy normalizer $c_{occ}=125$:

$$
o_t^{occupancy}=\frac{N_t-c_{occ}}{c_{occ}+1},\qquad
N_t=(c_{occ}+1)o_t^{occupancy}+c_{occ}.
$$

Thus $o_t^{occupancy}>0$ means $N_t>125$, not that the building has any
occupants. With this normalizer, $N_t>0$ corresponds to
$o_t^{occupancy}>-125/126$, up to numerical precision. Do not assume this
constant for an independently configured hidden environment without checking
its interface; none of the current controllers gates actions on this signal.

## 6. Policy outputs and hard action constraints

For $K$ air handlers (AHUs) and $V$ zones/VAV boxes:

$$
a_t\in[-1,1]^{3K+2+2V},\quad a_t\text{ has dtype float32},
\qquad
b_t\in\{0,1\}^{K+1},\quad b_t\text{ has dtype int32}. \tag{10}
$$

All outputs must be finite, have the current building's exact shapes, and use
the interface keys:

```python
{
    "continuous_action": a_t,
    "discrete_action": b_t,
}
```

Conceptually, the continuous commands are

$$
a_t\longleftrightarrow
\big((\tau_k,p_k,\rho_k)_{k=1}^{K},\tau_w,p_w,
      (d_i,v_i)_{i=1}^{V}\big).
$$

The binary commands switch $K$ AHUs and the shared hot-water system (pump and
heat source). Map commands through metadata; do not assume a new building uses
an inferred positional layout.

For a normalized scalar $u\in[-1,1]$, configured affine action conversion is

$$
q(u)=q_{\min}+\frac{u+1}{2}(q_{\max}-q_{\min}).
$$

| Command | Native conversion | Native command interval |
| --- | --- | --- |
| AHU supply temperature | $295+10u$ | $[285,305]\ {\rm K}$ |
| AHU static pressure | $1000+1000u$ | $[0,2000]\ {\rm Pa}$ |
| AHU recirculation | $(1+u)/2$ | $[0,1]$ before ventilation cap |
| Water supply temperature | $330+20u$ | $[310,350]\ {\rm K}$ |
| Water differential pressure | $10+10u$ | $[0,20]\ {\rm bar}$ |
| VAV damper | $(1+u)/2$ | $[0,1]$ before damper floor |
| VAV reheat valve | $(1+u)/2$ | $[0,1]$ |

Water pressure is in **bar**, not pascals or metres of head. For the shipped
ventilation settings, the effective controls are

$$
p_k^{\rm eff}=b_k\max(p_k,200\ {\rm Pa}),\qquad
\rho_k^{\rm eff}=\min(\rho_k,0.7),\qquad
d_i^{\rm eff}=\max(d_i,0.1). \tag{11}
$$

Occupancy does not impose $b_k=1$ as a hard action constraint. Turning an AHU off
is admissible. Continuous zero commands select midpoints of native ranges;
they do not mean all equipment is off.

Measured building-1 example:

$$
K=4,\quad V=39,\quad d=352,\quad \dim a=92,\quad\dim b=5.
$$

Zero-based indices: AHU-1 temperature/pressure/recirculation $=0,1,2$;
water temperature/pressure $=12,13$; VAV-1 damper/reheat $=14,15$.
`pressure_indices = [1, 4, 7, 10, 13]` includes water pressure;
`water_indices = [12]` denotes the water-temperature entry.

Sources: [action construction and normalizers](smart_buildings/evaluate.py),
[AHU](smart_buildings/smart_control/simulator/air_handler.py),
[VAV](smart_buildings/smart_control/simulator/vav.py),
[pump](smart_buildings/smart_control/simulator/pump.py).

## 7. Physical coupling and transition structure

The following equations describe components of the local transition $F_s$;
they do not make the complete hybrid, stateful simulator a linear system.

### 7.1 AHU mixing, cooling and airflow

For AHU $k$, return temperature $T_k^{\rm ret}$, outside temperature
$T^{\rm out}$, and effective recirculation $\rho_k$:

$$
T_k^{\rm mix}=\rho_k T_k^{\rm ret}+(1-\rho_k)T^{\rm out},
\qquad
T_k^{\rm ahu}=
\begin{cases}
\min(T_k^{\rm mix},\tau_k),&b_k=1,\\
T_k^{\rm mix},&b_k=0.
\end{cases} \tag{12}
$$

For zones $\mathcal Z_k$ assigned to AHU $k$:

$$
\widetilde\nu_i=
\max\left(d_i^{\rm eff}\nu_i^{\rm design}
\sqrt{\frac{p_k^{\rm eff}}{p_i^{\rm design}}},10^{-5}\right),
\quad
\lambda_k=\min\left(1,\frac{F_k^{\max}}
                              {\sum_{i\in\mathcal Z_k}\widetilde\nu_i}\right),
\quad\nu_i=\lambda_k\widetilde\nu_i. \tag{13}
$$

Typical shipped parameters: $p_i^{\rm design}=2000\ {\rm Pa}$,
$\nu_i^{\rm design}=2\ {\rm m^3/s}$, $F_k^{\max}=20\ {\rm m^3/s}$.
The AHU reports zero airflow when off; the VAV model retains its numerical
$10^{-5}\ {\rm m^3/s}$ demand floor. Do not infer exact zero flow at every
internal computation from the off flag.

For a running AHU with delivered flow $F_k$ and fan efficiency $\eta_k$:

$$
P_k^{\rm fans}=(2-\rho_k)\frac{F_kp_k^{\rm eff}}{\eta_k},
\qquad
P_k^{\rm cooling}=
\frac{\rho_a c_a F_k(T_k^{\rm mix}-T_k^{\rm ahu})}{\mathrm{COP}_k}. \tag{14}
$$

The temperature action bounds apply to $\tau_k$, not the actual mixed or
delivered temperature. In cold weather $T_k^{\rm ahu}<285\ {\rm K}$ is possible.

### 7.2 Water, reheat and zone heat delivery

For water supply temperature $T^w$ and zone water flow $q_i^w$:

$$
\epsilon_i^0=\min\left(1,
\frac{\rho_wc_wq_i^w}{\rho_ac_a\nu_i}\right),\qquad
T_i^{\rm sup}=T_k^{\rm ahu}+\epsilon_i(T^w-T_k^{\rm ahu}),
$$

$$
\epsilon_i=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle\min\left(\epsilon_i^0,
\max\left(0,\frac{308.15-T_k^{\rm ahu}}{T^w-T_k^{\rm ahu}}\right)\right),
&T^w>T_k^{\rm ahu},\\
\epsilon_i^0,&T^w\leq T_k^{\rm ahu}.
\end{cases} \tag{15}
$$

The $308.15\ {\rm K}=35^\circ{\rm C}$ cap limits added reheat; it does not
actively cool inlet air already hotter than the cap. The full VAV code also
handles equipment run states.

$$
Q_i^{\rm HVAC}=\rho_ac_a\nu_i(T_i^{\rm sup}-T_i^{\rm zone}). \tag{16}
$$

With valve flow coefficients $K_{v,i}=0.108v_i$ in the shipped configuration,
shared header resistance $R_h$, and water pressure $p_w$ in bar:

$$
K_v=\sum_i K_{v,i},\quad
q^w=\frac{K_v}{3600}\sqrt{\frac{p_w}{1+K_v^2R_h}},\quad
q_i^w=q^w\frac{K_{v,i}}{K_v}\quad(K_v>0). \tag{17}
$$

For $K_v=0$, all water flows are zero. The $1/3600$ converts the valve relation's
cubic metres per hour to cubic metres per second. When the pump runs:

$$
P^{\rm pump}=\frac{q^w\rho_wg h_w}{\eta_w}
=\frac{q^w\,10^5p_w}{\eta_w}.
$$

### 7.3 Implicit thermal update

Let $T^{\rm cv}$ contain the solved control-volume temperatures;
$P,S\in\mathbb R^{n_{\rm cv}\times V}$ distribute zone heat and select sensed
zone temperatures; and $G=\operatorname{diag}(\rho_ac_a\nu_i)$. A thermal solve
has the structure

$$
\left(A_t+P G_t S^\top\right)T_{t+1}^{\rm cv}
=f_t+P G_t T_t^{\rm sup},\qquad
A_t=\frac{C^{\rm cv}}{\Delta t}+K_t+D^{\rm mass}. \tag{18}
$$

$C^{\rm cv}$ is thermal capacitance; $K_t$ contains conduction and boundary
exchange; $f_t$ includes old-temperature, ambient, internal-gain, and retained
mass contributions. The simulator also applies convection and equipment/water
dynamics. Equation (18) is one component of the transition, not a claim that all
disturbances and coefficients are constant across steps.

For an interior-mass node $j$ with capacitance $C_j^m$, coupling $g_j^m$ and
temperature $M_j$:

$$
\frac{C_j^m}{\Delta t}(M_{j,t+1}-M_{j,t})
=g_j^m(T_{j,t+1}^{\rm cv}-M_{j,t+1}),
\qquad
D_j^{\rm mass}=\frac{(C_j^m/\Delta t)g_j^m}{C_j^m/\Delta t+g_j^m}.
$$

The mass contribution is zero where no mass node exists.

Sources: [AHU dynamics](smart_buildings/smart_control/simulator/air_handler.py),
[VAV and reheat](smart_buildings/smart_control/simulator/vav.py),
[water hydraulics](smart_buildings/smart_control/simulator/hot_water_system.py),
[pump power](smart_buildings/smart_control/simulator/pump.py),
[thermal solver](smart_buildings/smart_control/simulator/finite_difference_solver.py),
[simulator sequencing](smart_buildings/smart_control/simulator/simulator_flexible_floor_plan.py).

## 8. Mathematical difficulties a model must address

**Partial observation:**

$$
h_s(x)=h_s(x')\quad\text{can hold while}\quad
h_s(F_s(x,u,w))\ne h_s(F_s(x',u,w)).
$$

Identical current observations can hide different wall/mass states; memory,
state estimation or adaptation can therefore change the optimal action.

**Delayed effects:**

$$
\frac{\partial\ell_{t+h}}{\partial u_t}\ne0\quad(h>0)
$$

where derivatives exist. Minimizing only $\ell_t$ can sacrifice future comfort
or miss opportunities for preconditioning.

**Shared equipment:**

$$
i,j\in\mathcal Z_k\ \Longrightarrow\quad
T_{i}^{\rm inlet}=T_{j}^{\rm inlet}=T_k^{\rm ahu}.
$$

Zone-specific dampers/reheat must resolve incompatible local needs under a
shared AHU supply state, airflow capacity, and shared water system.

**Saturation and locally inactive commands:** for running equipment,

$$
\begin{aligned}
u_{\rm fan}\leq-0.8&\Longrightarrow p^{\rm eff}=200\ {\rm Pa},\\
u_{\rm recirc}\geq0.4&\Longrightarrow\rho^{\rm eff}=0.7,\\
u_{\rm damper}\leq-0.8&\Longrightarrow d^{\rm eff}=0.1,\\
T^{\rm mix}<285\ {\rm K}&\Longrightarrow
\partial T^{\rm ahu}/\partial\tau=0
\quad\text{throughout the allowed temperature-command range}.
\end{aligned}
$$

At equality the piecewise map can have a kink. For example, with
$T^{\rm ret}=293\ {\rm K}$ and $\rho\leq0.7$:

$$
T^{\rm out}\leq\frac{285-0.7(293)}{0.3}
=266.\overline3\ {\rm K}\simeq-6.82^\circ{\rm C}
\ \Longrightarrow\ T^{\rm mix}\leq285\ {\rm K}.
$$

All allowed AHU temperature setpoints then give the same supply temperature;
other actuators must determine heating and ventilation behavior.

**Hybrid search size:**

$$
\#\{\text{binary open-loop schedules}\}=2^{(K+1)H}.
$$

For building 1 and $H=8640$, this is $2^{43200}$ binary schedules plus
$8640\times92=794880$ continuous open-loop coordinates. A structured feedback
policy avoids enumerating that space.

**Transfer and variable dimensions:**

$$
(K,V,d,\text{geometry},\text{materials},\text{climate})_s
\ne(K,V,d,\text{geometry},\text{materials},\text{climate})_{s'}.
$$

Use metadata to construct a controller for the current building. Performance on
one building/climate does not establish transfer performance.

## 9. Model-design and evaluation contract

The target is a causal policy solving

$$
\boxed{
\min_{\pi\in\Pi_{\rm causal}}
\mathbb E_{s\sim p_{\rm test}}
\left[\frac1{H_s}\sum_{t=0}^{H_s-1}
\left(0.6C_{s,t}+0.2E_{s,t}+0.2G_{s,t}\right)\right]
}\tag{19}
$$

subject to (1), (2), and (10), using the simulator's effective actuator mapping.
The distribution $p_{\rm test}$ is unknown. The empirical scenario mean in (3)
is the observed evaluation counterpart. A worst-case scenario score is useful
diagnostically; it is not the official mean objective.

For each new model, record:

1. Its policy/state representation $(\pi_\theta,z_t)$ and available information
   under (1)–(2).

Begin from the basic submitted policy, obtain the scored result, inspect its
failures, and use those failures to select the next change. Preserve the
competition reward and score during policy comparisons. A surrogate training
loss must be explicitly identified as a surrogate.

### Noninterference of evaluation diagnostics

The boiler's temperature getter mutates its tank temperature on each read at
nonzero elapsed time. A repeated same-timestamp probe returned 332.5 K and then
335.0 K. Diagnostic code must reuse the returned observation, and capture the
reward response produced inside `env.step`, rather than adding sensor/reward
queries. Protocol `non_mutating_v2` was verified against an ordinary rollout for
exact observations, actions and rewards. Earlier instrumented results are
historical hypothesis evidence only and cannot qualify a candidate submission.
This corrects the measurement procedure, not the competition simulator itself.
